Emojify

digital · 2025
ClientSelf-initiated
LocationBerlin
Year2025
DepartmentDigital
TypologySpec project, tool
ProgramImage, GIF, video, and webcam mosaic generator
ScopeConcept, Algorithm, Engineering, Deployment
StackRuby (CLI), Python · Flask (web app), ffmpeg, Docker, Railway
Emoji are the closest thing to a universal pictographic vocabulary that has ever existed. Unicode standardises them, every major OS renders them, and the same handful of glyphs carry meaning across languages, age groups, and contexts that share almost nothing else. They have become a default register of online speech, with their own grammar, their own ambiguities, and their own slow drift in meaning over time. Treating them as a quantization palette is a small inversion: a vocabulary developed for compression of feeling, repurposed as a vocabulary for compression of pixels. The output is a familiar image rendered in a deliberately limited language, with whatever survives that translation foregrounded as the legible part.
The implementation is straightforward. Each input frame is reduced to a low-resolution grid, and each cell is mapped to the emoji whose mean RGB is closest in Euclidean distance across a precomputed lookup of every renderable Unicode glyph. RGB-mean is a coarse metric that ignores texture and luminance, but it produces stable matches across renderers that draw the same glyph slightly differently across Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Twemoji. A second pass flood-fills adjacent cells that resolved to the same glyph and merges them into larger glyphs at 2×, 4×, or 8× the base size, so flat regions collapse into bigger emoji while detailed regions stay at base resolution. Inputs are normalised through ImageMagick, video and webcam streams are decomposed frame-by-frame through ffmpeg, the CLI is Ruby, the web app is Python Flask, containerised through Docker and deployed on Railway. The tool is called Emojify.
